1. Introduction

Switzerland is a landlocked, mountainous country in the heart of Europe, famous for its dramatic Alpine landscapes, crystal-clear lakes, charming towns, and a deeply rooted tradition of precision and neutrality. It mixes natural beauty with modern sophistication: from glacial peaks to cosmopolitan cities, from Swiss chocolate and cheese to world-class public transport.

As a travel destination, Switzerland appeals to many kinds of travelers: adventure seekers (skiing, hiking), culture lovers (historic towns, galleries), and wellness tourists (spas, lakeside retreats).

Sources: Encyclopedia Britannica.

2. Location & Geography

Location: Switzerland sits in Central Europe, bordering France (west), Germany (north), Italy (south), and Austria and Liechtenstein (east).

Size: Around 41,285 km².

Terrain / Regions

  • The Alps: About 60% of Switzerland’s surface is mountainous, with many peaks over 4,000 m.
  • Swiss Plateau (Mittelland): Rolling hills, plains, and lakes — where much of the population lives.
  • Jura Mountains: A smaller chain to the northwest.

Elevation Extremes

Highest point: Dufourspitze (Monte Rosa) at ~4,634 m. Lowest point: Lake Maggiore at around 193–195 m above sea level.

Hydrography & Time Zone

Switzerland has numerous lakes (e.g., Lake Geneva, Lake Neuchâtel) and important rivers (Rhine, Rhône). Time zone: Central European Time (CET, UTC+1), with daylight saving time in summer.

Sources: about.ch, indexmundi.com, infcis.iaea.org, Wikipedia.

3. Language

Switzerland has four national languages: German, French, Italian, and Romansh.

Breakdown of usage

  • German: ~62% (including Swiss German dialects).
  • French: ~23% (western cantons).
  • Italian: ~8% (mainly Ticino).
  • Romansh: ~0.5% (parts of Graubünden).

Official status: German, French, and Italian are official at the federal level; Romansh is a national language used for communication with Romansh speakers.

Traveler tips: English is commonly spoken in tourist areas. Learn a few local greetings: German “Grüezi”, French “Bonjour”, Italian “Buongiorno”.

Sources: Wikipedia, SWI swissinfo.ch, Federal Department of Foreign Affairs.

4. Currency

The currency is the Swiss Franc (CHF).

Credit and debit cards are widely accepted; ATMs are common. Switzerland is relatively expensive — expect higher costs for accommodation, food, transport, and alpine activities.

Sources: WorldAtlas.

5. Transportation

Swiss Travel System: Very efficient — trains, buses, trams, and boats connect cities, mountains, and villages.

Mountain transport: Cable cars, funiculars, and cog railways are common for scenic access to hikes and ski areas.

City transport: Many Swiss cities have developed tram and bus networks. Consider a travel pass (e.g., Swiss Travel Pass) for unlimited or discounted travel.

6. Climate & Best Time to Visit

Climate varies greatly with altitude: mountains are cold; the plateau is temperate.

  • Winter (Dec–Mar): Cold and snowy — ideal for winter sports.
  • Spring / Early Summer (May–June): Wildflowers, fewer crowds — great for hiking.
  • High Summer (July–Aug): Warm on the plateau; good for hiking and lake activities.
  • Autumn (Sep–Oct): Fall colours and quieter villages.

Sources: indexmundi.com.

7. Entry Requirements

Switzerland is part of the Schengen Area; Schengen visa rules apply. For stays up to 90 days in a 180-day period, many nationalities use a Schengen visa. Citizens of EU/EFTA countries generally do not need a visa for short visits.

Travel insurance: Highly recommended. Consider coverage for mountain activities if you plan to hike or ski.

Sources: Swiss Federal Department of Foreign Affairs (EDA), help.myswitzerland.com.
